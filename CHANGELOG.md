# Changelog

All notable changes to the Flock Off Cobb site are recorded here.

## 2026-08-25

### Added
- The event bars carry Cobb County PD's four community town halls on Flock: Thu Aug 27 at Milford Recreation Center, Mon Aug 31 at Sewell Mill Library, Tue Sep 1 at West Cobb Senior Center, and Tue Sep 15 at North Cobb Senior Center, all 6:30–8 PM. The bars had been empty since the Live Info Session retired on the 19th. They are one series run four times, so `name` is shared and the venue is what tells them apart.
- The strip now prints `where`. It was a declared field that nothing had ever rendered, which was fine when one event was on the bar and is not when four share a name and a time and differ only by address.
- A card for events with no poster. Most events are not announced with a flyer, and these four were a paragraph of prose, so an event without artwork now opens to what the strip has no room for: what the meeting is, the venue, the street address, and the date and time. The frame moved from the image to the sheet so the links bar sits inside it as a footer rather than floating below the shadow.
- The card repeats the date and time the bar's title already carries, on purpose. The title is one ellipsised line and a long name eats the time first: at 390px "Cobb PD Flock Town Hall · Tue Sep 15, 6:30–8 PM" truncates mid-time, and under reduced motion that title is the whole strip. A poster prints its times in the artwork; a card has to do the same job.

### Changed
- The town hall description drops two words. "Flock Safety" is the company's own branding rather than its name, so it is just Flock now; and "safeguarded" is the exact claim the campaign exists to dispute, so we are not the ones asserting it on their behalf. The line is our paraphrase of the county's announcement, not a quotation, so it is ours to word: "Cobb County PD will address common concerns about Flock, explain how the technology is used, and take questions from the community."
- Clicking either bar now opens the whole bill: all four town halls listed in date order with countdown, date, time, venue and street address, rather than the one event the click happened to land on. A lap of four is 108 seconds, so any one hall is on screen about a quarter of the time, and "click the one you want" meant waiting for it to come round on a strip that is moving. Reading four dates and picking one is what a reader wanted to do anyway.
- `eventAt()` and its hit-testing are gone with it, along with `byId`, the `clip()` accessor they used, and `current`, which turned out to be written in three places and read in none. The `.ebar-ev` segments stay, because they are what the tape is built from and what carries each event's `data-cd`, but nothing hit-tests them now. The tape still stops on `pointerdown` and the reason has changed: not aiming any more, just that a strip moving under a finger reads as something that got away from you.
- The soonest event that has not finished yet is accented: red rule, red countdown, red venue, so a list of four dates does not need reading end to end to find the one that matters today. `nextUp()` returns null rather than falling back to the first row once every event on the bar is over and only lingering, so nothing is accented in that window. Checked at six instants including 9 PM on a hall night, where the finished meeting stays listed as "Just happened" and the accent has correctly moved to the next one, and after the last hall, where nothing is accented and the title falls back through `titleEvent()`.
- A `note` shared by every live event is hoisted to the top of the panel as an introduction instead of printing on all four rows. Only when they all match, so a mixed bill keeps each note with its own event.
- A poster keeps everything it had. It renders inside its own row in an `.ebar-shot` wrapper, which is the positioned ancestor the `linksAt` overlay needs, so a flyer still gets framed artwork with its buttons parked on its own band. Events without artwork put their links under the address instead, as the same button inverted for paper.

### Changed
- Events now drop out of the panel as well as the tape as they retire, and an open panel keeps up with the clock instead of going stale or being destroyed. The bar and the speed already handled this: `live()` drops the event, the 30-second tick rebuilds, and `pace()` re-derives the lap so three halls scroll at the same 36 px/s four did. `linger` stays at 4 hours, so a hall that ends at 8 PM is still listed as "Just happened" until midnight for anyone arriving late.
- The next-up accent is moved by `paintCountdowns()` rather than set once when the panel is filled. It had to be: a panel left open across 8 PM on a hall night showed that row's countdown flip to "Just happened" while it stayed painted red as the one to plan around. The accent and the countdown are two readings of the same clock and now move on the same pass, which costs one class toggle on a pass that was already running.
- The tick calls `refresh()`, which rebuilds a closed bar and refreshes an open one in place. `build()` replaces the bar's innerHTML, which takes the panel with it and clears `is-open`, so an open dropdown would have slammed shut at the exact moment the list changed under the reader. Open, it swaps only the tape's runs and the panel's contents, and the panel's height transition runs from the old height to the new one so the retired row is seen to go rather than vanishing between frames.

### Fixed
- **The pace was measured before the countdowns were painted, so the tape sped up every time an event retired.** `runHTML()` writes the countdown spans empty and `paintCountdowns()` is what puts "In 3 days" in them, so the run is not its final width until it has run. Pacing first sized the lap for a tape narrower than the one on screen. On first load this was invisible because the `fonts.ready` re-pace corrects it a moment later; on a retirement there is nothing to correct it, and it measured 39.4 px/s instead of 36 once the first hall dropped off. Both `build()` and `refresh()` now pace after painting. Found only by running the clock across the boundary, which is why the recipe for doing that is now written down in the README rather than left as "override Date somehow".
- The tape scrolled roughly six times too fast the moment it had four events on it, and it was the stylesheet's fixed 22.6s lap that did it. Lap time is only the same thing as speed while the run is a constant width: the tape covers its own width once per lap, so a run four events long in the same 22.6s moves four times as far per second. Measured on the page: the single Live Info Session ran a 630px lap at 27.9 px/s, and the four town halls ran a 3,886px lap at 172 px/s. The duration is now derived from the measured run width against a constant `TAPE_SPEED`, so adding a fifth event lengthens the lap instead of speeding up the type. Set to 36 px/s, a little quicker than the old bar's 27.9, which read as slow once four events meant waiting through all of them to come round. Verified at exactly 36.0 px/s on both bars at desktop width (108s lap) and at 390px where the type shrinks and the lap shortens with it, which is the case a fixed duration would have got wrong in the other direction. `TAPE_SPEED` is the one number to turn; everything else follows from it at every width.
- Re-paced on resize, because the type shrinks at the 520px breakpoint and a rotation would otherwise change the speed, and again on `document.fonts.ready`, because Space Mono lands after first paint and is wider than the fallback that the build-time measurement would have used.
- Worth knowing before adding a fifth: a longer bill means a longer lap, and at four events a full cycle is about 2m19s, so any one hall is on screen for about a quarter of it. That is survivable here because the run is built in date order and the tape starts at `translateX(0)`, so a fresh page load opens on the soonest event. It stops being survivable at eight.
- The venue headline on the card rendered invisible, paper on paper. `.ebar-card` set no `color`, so it inherited the bar's light-on-ink type against the card's paper background. `note` and `address` escaped it only because they each happen to set their own colour. Caught on screen, not by reading: the space was there and the words were not.

### Verified
- Retirement is 4 hours after each `end`, so a hall that finishes at 8 PM drops at midnight and the next one moves up on its own. Checked against the real data at ten instants: now, the morning of the first, during it, after it ends, a minute before it retires, half an hour after, the day before the second, during the third, after the last one ends, and past the last retirement where the bars hide themselves. Every weekday printed on the strip matches the weekday the declared `[y, monthIndex, day]` actually lands on, which is the cross-check that catches an off-by-one month.
- Both bars, desktop and at 390px in `dev/mobile-preview.html`. One note for next time: the extension's first synthetic click after a navigation does not reach the page, which reads exactly like a broken bar. A dispatched `MouseEvent` opened it first try and a real click logged all four of pointerdown/mousedown/mouseup/click and opened it, so the bar is fine and the harness is what needs the second click.

### Added
- A recent-posts strip inside step 5 of Join the Campaign, four cards driven by `assets/posts.json`. The newest four entries with a caption render; the rest stay in the file and do not. A missing, empty or malformed file hides the strip and leaves step 5 exactly as it was, so it is safe to leave broken.
- `dev/add-post.py` puts a post on it: reads the post's public embed page, pulls the picture, its dimensions and whether it is a carousel or a reel, writes a 640px WebP into `assets/posts/`, and inserts or updates the entry keyed on the post URL. Re-running keeps any caption and alt not re-supplied. Four posts came to 224KB against roughly 1.1MB of originals.
- `--caption` and `--alt` are deliberately not scraped. The card wants a trimmed line rather than the full post with hashtags, and alt text is worth a human sentence. **Describe the picture, not the post's subject**: the first pass here got three of four wrong by paraphrasing what each post was about, which is no use to somebody who cannot see that one card is a drawing of two arms holding a WARNING placard.
- It is a hand-run helper, not a pipeline, and the README says so. One post, when asked, from a personal machine, for the campaign's own account. A scheduled job hitting Instagram from a datacenter IP is the version that risks the account. Instagram can reshape the embed page whenever it likes and this will break; the fallback is saving a picture by hand and editing `posts.json`, which is plain data a human can always write.
- Why hand-curated at all: the account is a personal one, and Instagram's Basic Display API, the last that could read those, shut off in September 2024. Its replacement needs the account converted to Business or Creator, which is not this repo's call. An embed widget needs no conversion but puts Meta's tracking in front of every visitor, on a site whose argument is that unconsented tracking is wrong and which runs GoatCounter for exactly that reason.

### Fixed
- Two colours from the dark step box bled onto the paper cards: `.step p` is pale cream and `.step a` is yellow, and both beat a bare `.igcap` or `.igcard` on specificity, so captions came out washed-out grey and the card text was yellow on cream. Every colour on the card is now stated and the selectors are two classes deep. Second time this trap has been hit, after `.ebar-card`'s invisible venue headline.
- The picture box is a fixed 4:5 with `object-fit:cover`. Posts arrive in whatever shape the app gave them and this strip mixes 4:5 stills with 9:16 reels, which is nearly half again as tall at the same width; left at their own ratios the captions stop lining up and the row grows to the tallest thing in it. Cropping to one shape is what Instagram's own grid does.

### Fixed
- On a real iPhone the event bar would not close by tapping the strip that opened it: it closed and reopened in one gesture. Real touch hardware delivers a second click from the same tap, and the two ways through the click handler were not symmetric about it. A second click on the poster falls out at the `.ebar-btn` guard and does nothing, which is why closing by tapping the poster looked fine, while a second click on the strip itself lands on the open branch and toggles straight back. One tap is now one state change: every state change stamps a clock and clicks arriving inside 450ms of one are the gesture's own echo and are dropped, which covers Escape and outside-clicks too. Worth knowing for next time: no amount of synthetic tapping reproduces this. Playwright's WebKit delivers exactly one click per `tap()`, and the bug only showed up by dispatching the pairs by hand, where two clicks up to 300ms apart reopened it every time.

## 2026-08-14

### Added
- An event bar for dated events, directly under the nav, carrying the DeFlock Cobb Live Info Session on Tue Aug 18. It is there because that is the only place on the page that is high on a phone. Measured in WebKit at iPhone 13 size, the hero art and the flyer fill the entire first screen, so the h1 is already at 1.23 screens and the existing ticker at 1.96, which puts anything after the hero two full swipes down. Under the nav is y=60 at every width. It scrolls away with the page rather than sticking, since a second permanently pinned band would cost 44px of a 664px viewport forever.
- It is built for more than one event from the start. The tape is generated from an `EVENTS` array and each event is its own segment, so which poster opens depends on which segment the click landed on: a segment under the pointer wins outright, and a click that lands in a gap falls to whichever segment has the most of itself on screen. That is also why the tape stops dead on `pointerdown` rather than only on hover. A click has to be able to mean the thing that was under it, and a target moving at 30 seconds a lap cannot be aimed at fairly, least of all with a finger on a phone where there is no hover to stop it first. With one event it always answers the same way; with several the moment of the click is what chooses. Verified with a second event injected into a throwaway copy: clicking inside each segment opened that event's poster and title.
- When an event ends and when the bar stops carrying it are two separate instants. Each entry has a `linger` in hours, set to 6 here, so the info session finishing at 10 PM stays up until 4 AM for anyone arriving late that night, and the tape says "Just happened" rather than continuing to claim it is happening now. Conflating the two is exactly how an event that finished at ten reads as live at two in the morning. Checked by freezing the page clock at seven instants across the week: four days out, the night before, the morning of, during, just after the end, ten minutes before retiring and ten minutes after.
- To add an event, add an entry. To retire one, do nothing and let its `linger` run out. The bar drops events as they finish, rebuilds the tape, and hides itself once nothing is left, so the page returns to normal without anyone editing it.
- Opening is the tape running out: the strip accelerates off to the left as though the reel ended, and what was behind it is the poster. The panel is in the flow rather than overlaid, which makes this an accordion instead of a modal, with no scrim and no focus trap to get wrong, and the very top of the document is the least harmful place there is to push from. The chevron that was drifting to say the bar could be clicked stops breathing and turns to point back up at it.
- The countdown is days only and scrolls with the event rather than sitting pinned, and it counts whole Eastern calendar days rather than 24-hour chunks, so the day before reads "Tomorrow" all evening instead of flipping to "Today" at some arbitrary hour. Same DST-correct `zoned()` helper as the council countdowns, because two clocks on one page must not disagree about what day it is.

## 2026-08-13

### Added
- iPhones get the morph too, driven from script since the CSS property doesn't exist there. The two shapes carry the same 32 points in the same order — the condition that made them interpolable to CSS in the first place — so interpolating them by hand is the same arithmetic written out. Each path's rolled shape comes off its own `d` attribute and its open shape off the custom property the CSS rule would have used, so there is still one definition of each and `dev/morph.py` remains the only thing that writes them. When the loop is running it sets `js-morph`, which puts back everything the fallback had changed: the outline becomes the frame again, the window drops its own, the burst follows the box rather than fading, and the clip returns. The fallback is now only what is left if script never arrives at all.
- One implementation of that covers both engines, which is why the shape is routed through a custom property: browsers with the CSS `d` property read `--burst-d`, since an attribute would lose to the stylesheet there, and WebKit has no such property so reads the attribute, where the custom property means nothing. Script writes both every frame. The burst's shadow offset rides the same clock — at a resting 6px on a 17px dot it reads as a second dot beside the first rather than as a shadow, so it starts at 1px and opens out as the shape does.
- That loop reads its position back from the window's height each frame rather than running a second clock alongside the stylesheet's. The height is a fact about what is on screen rather than a prediction, so nothing can drift out of step: the open, the quiet close, the pop with its 125ms hold, and reduced motion collapsing all of it are each just a different way for that height to move, and all come out right without being special-cased. It idles out after about a dozen still frames and any interaction restarts it.

### Fixed
- Tapping the open flyer on an iPhone would start to shrink it and then leave it open, with no pop. `:focus-visible` was the cause: it is the platform's own answer to "was that a keyboard or a pointer", and the engines disagree — WebKit matches it after a tap on a button, Chrome does not. So the closing tap focused the button, the focus rule reasserted the open state a moment after the class came off, and `stillOpen()` agreed with the stylesheet that the flyer was still open, so nothing popped. This is the third time this bug has arrived in a different costume: `activeElement` first, then `:focus-within`, then `:focus-visible`, each fix verified in Chrome, where the difference cannot show. The browser is no longer asked. The last input before a focus decides whether it counts as keyboard — the same heuristic the property is meant to implement — and it now gives one answer everywhere.
- With Reduce Motion turned on, the flyer went dead to the touch for over two seconds after every close. The reform is what ends the pop, and it declines to run under reduced motion — there is nothing to grow out of when the burst is simply back — so nothing removed `is-popping`, and every open-state selector is gated on `:not(.is-popping)`. Taps in that window did nothing at all, which reads as the flyer being broken rather than as an animation being skipped. `startReform()` now reports whether it has taken ownership of ending the pop, and when it hasn't the pop is ended promptly instead. The backstop for a stalled rAF also came down from 2.2s to 1.4s, the margin it needed when an `animationend` ended the pop being gone.
- The fallback's `:hover` rules went behind the same `@media(hover:hover) and (min-width:861px)` guard the real open-state rules are behind. Without it the flyer broke on a phone after a single tap: iOS keeps `:hover` stuck on whatever was tapped last, so the unguarded rules matched a flyer that was closed — the sheet stayed shut, its own open rules being correctly inside the media query, while the burst was held at opacity 0 and the empty window kept its frame. What was left was a bordered box where the button used to be, on every tap after the first. Found by running the page through real WebKit rather than reasoning about it; the same run confirms `CSS.supports('d', …)` is false there, so the fallback is what an iPhone actually gets.
- The flyer was invisible on iPhones. WebKit does not implement the CSS `d` property, and the three morphing paths carried no `d` attribute at all — their geometry existed only in the stylesheet. So on any browser on an iPhone the burst had no shape to draw and the clipPath was empty, and an empty clipPath clips everything: the poster was gone too. What survived was the burst's label, which is HTML text, and the droplets, which are HTML elements. The rolled shape is now a plain `d` attribute on each path, which is the floor every browser gets; CSS only ever moves it from there.
- Where the CSS `d` property is missing the flyer now falls back to what it was before the morph rather than to a half-drawn version of it: the window carries a real frame of its own, the burst stays at its rolled size and fades out as the sheet unrolls instead of stretching into a poster-sized star, and the clip comes off, its only job having been to let the artwork up early behind a jagged edge that no longer moves. The frame is painted only when open, or the rolled state would be a paper rectangle sitting over the burst. Everything else — the pop, the droplets, the dwell, the dismiss latch — is class and HTML work and behaves the same either way. `dev/no-d.html` renders that branch in Chrome so it can be looked at without a device.

Starting the moment the sheet vanished read as the surroundings being in a hurry to get on with it; an eighth of a second of nothing first is what makes the gap feel vacated rather than reclaimed. The width and tilt of the button wait on the same beat, so the geometry is still home by 385ms, comfortably before the burst fades back in at 600ms. The soft close overrides this and does not hesitate — it has no droplets to wait for.
- Clicking the flyer pops it on a desktop, not just tapping it on a phone. Two things had to change for that. Hover has usually already opened the sheet by the time a click lands, so "is it open" can't just be the `is-open` class — without the hover half of that test the first click would set a class that changed nothing on screen and it would take a second click to pop. And the pointer is still on the flyer afterwards, so `:hover` alone would hold the sheet open through the pop and spring it back the instant the pop class lifted, making the click look like it had done nothing; an `is-dismissed` latch takes the hover rules out until the pointer actually leaves. Keyboard focus deliberately still opens a dismissed flyer, and the latch is only set when the pointer is genuinely on it, so Enter on a focused button doesn't switch hover off for a pointer that was never there.
- A click also skips the 250ms dwell, on the desktop path only. The dwell guards against a pointer sweeping through the hero firing the whole performance, and a click is not an accident. It also has to be skipped for the click to pop at all, which is the same shape of bug that stopped taps working on a phone: clicking a `<button>` focuses it, `focusin` fires before `click` and marks the flyer as freshly opened, so the very click that closes it reset the dwell clock to zero a moment before the handler read it — 250ms of hover then a click soft-closed every time. A phone keeps its dwell: there a tap is both the open and the close, so a fumbled double-tap should still close quietly, and the opening tap has already focused the button so the closing one fires no second `focusin`.
- A close is now one-shot. `openedAt` is cleared when a close is committed and a close with nothing open returns early, so the `mouseleave` that eventually follows a click-to-close doesn't find a stale clock and fire a second burst of droplets over an empty space.
- Only letting go of the flyer pops it — the pointer leaving it, or a tap on the flyer itself. Escape and a tap elsewhere on the page now dismiss it with the quiet .25s close, since a burst of droplets across the hero in response to a tap somewhere else reads as something misfiring rather than as the flyer closing.
- The sheet is still not part of the pop. Artwork, frame and shadow all leave on the frame it closes, and the droplets play over the space they occupied, so nothing is ever seen getting smaller — by the time anything moves there is nothing left to see. An earlier pass faded the sheet at full size over 90ms and snapped the geometry at 100ms behind that cover, which does avoid a shrink but leaves the poster hanging around for the first third of its own pop, and that reads as a fade rather than a burst.
- Driven from script, since the collapsed state is also the state the page loads in and a CSS rule alone would fire it on every page load: a class goes on only when something that was open has just closed, and since three separate things can hold the flyer open (hover, keyboard focus, the tap class), all three are checked first. Cleared again on re-entry, plus on a timer in case the tab is hidden mid-pop and the animation never reports finishing.
- That class also carries the closing transitions, which means it has to be on the element before they start, and that turned out to be delicate in two ways worth remembering: the reflow that replays an already-running pop has to sit inside its own branch rather than in front of everything, and hover is tracked with a flag rather than read back with `matches(':hover')`, because that call flushes style. Either mistake leaves the pop playing over a sheet still rolling up behind it at the old pace. For the same reason every property that changes on the way out is named explicitly in those rules; an omitted one keeps the base transition and drags.

### Fixed
- The flyer no longer fights itself on its own edge. Parking the cursor near the right edge made it flicker open and shut, and leaving it downward onto the hero buttons made it shrink instead of popping. Both were the same cause: the tilt lived on `.flyer`, which is the element deciding `:hover`, so the hit region changed shape with the state. Rotating the closed box about its top-right corner swings the right edge about 5px further right than the open box ever reaches, so a cursor in that sliver hovers while closed and misses once open — 320 sample points of it, all at x 472–476. And closing snaps the geometry at 100ms while animating the tilt for 260ms, so a full-size box spends that first stretch rotating back, sweeping its bottom edge down over a band roughly 9px deep across the entire width, exactly where those buttons are; the cursor you just left with gets covered again, `mouseenter` fires, the pop class is stripped and the close falls back to the plain transitions, which is a shrink.
- The tilt therefore moved to `.flyer-btn`, and an unrotated pad (`.flyer::before`, inset -12px, always larger than the rotated button in either state) is now what decides `:hover`. Both scans read 0 afterwards, against a control that still reports the old numbers. `dev/hover-stability.js` reruns them; worth doing after any change to the flyer's transform, anchoring, `--fw-open`, the pad inset or the closing timings. The lasting rule: anything that changes the flyer's shape belongs on the button or below, never on `.flyer`.
- The pop's timings moved out of the `.is-popping` class and into the closed state, which is where a close reads them from regardless. Leaving the flyer downward onto the hero buttons still shrank behind the droplets after the tilt fix: the class carried the closing transitions, so it had to land before the first style flush after `:hover` dropped, and on that path it didn't. Closing by tap or Escape was always fine, because those change a class and both changes land in one recalc. Rather than hunt for whichever listener was flushing style first, the dependency is gone — a transition reads its timing from the state it is heading into, so the closed rules are now the closing behaviour and the open rules the opening one. Closing with the class never added at all now gives identical timings to closing with it, verified both ways. The class is left doing one job: holding the rolled burst back until the sheet has gone, and adding the droplets.
- The base `--dur` dropped to .14s, and is now only a fallback for anything not named explicitly.

### Changed
- The jagged edge now becomes the flyer's edge. Opening used to fade the burst out while a rectangle grew in behind it, and the moment the shape changed read as a jolt. One path draws the outline the whole way through instead: the comic burst while rolled, the sheet's own frame when open, and every shape between, with the spikes retracting along their own rays into the four edges. The window consequently never draws a frame at all — its border is a transparent 4px spacer that insets the artwork off the outline, since a real one would double the frame at the end and give away that two different things had been drawn.
- Three things that had to line up for that, all of them easy to get wrong later: the open shape carries the same 32 points in the same order as the burst, because CSS `d` will not interpolate otherwise, with the nearest point to each corner snapped onto it so the rectangle doesn't end up chamfered (`dev/morph.py` generates the pair); the burst's box became the window's box rather than an oversized one of its own, so the viewBox maps onto the sheet exactly and the open path is simply the viewBox rectangle, with the spikes overhanging by leaving it; and the `drop-shadow` filter came off, replaced by a second copy of the path painted behind and offset 6px in CSS pixels. That last one matters twice over: the shape is redrawn every frame now and a filter over the whole open sheet is expensive enough to make that stutter, and a CSS-pixel offset stays a true 6px matching `--shadow` instead of being squeezed as the box goes from a wide band to a tall sheet.
- The flyer's artwork now appears while the edge is still morphing, instead of after it has finished. It is clipped to the very shape the outline is drawing — a second copy of the same two paths in objectBoundingBox units, morphing on the same clock — so it can be revealed through the burst as the spikes retract rather than spilling past them. Being a rectangle inside a jagged outline, it previously had to wait for the shape to be nearly rectangular before it dared fade in: it started at 260ms of a 500ms open and was not full until 470ms, which is why it felt like it turned up after the animation was over. It now starts at 60ms and is full by 250ms. Note this means three copies of the shape are kept in step — the outline, its shadow, and the clip — all emitted by `dev/morph.py`.
- The burst's label is pinned to the rolled band rather than centred in the burst, or it would ride the middle of the sheet downward as the box grows, and it fades in the first 30%.

### Fixed
- The unroll was choppy. The sheet was laid out at the rolled width and grown to the open width, which makes the browser rescale a 1080×1350 JPEG on every frame of the animation. It is now laid out at the open width and scaled down while rolled, so the image is rastered once and the compositor does the scaling. The scale is derived from the same width variable, so the sheet still sits flush inside its frame the whole way rather than drifting against it.
- The burst was being redrawn every frame too: its box stretched across the button, and the button's width is the thing that animates, so its stretched path and drop-shadow were re-rendered continuously through an unroll it is only fading out of. Its width is now pinned to the rolled width, which is the only width it is ever visible at.

### Changed
- The unroll runs on one clock and one curve. Width, height, sheet and burst each had their own duration and easing (.3s to .6s), which reads as choppy even when no frames are dropped; they now share `--dur` and `--ease`, with the open state declaring .5s.

- Tapping the open flyer on a phone now pops it. Two separate things a tap does that a hover doesn't were both breaking it, and neither is visible on desktop. First, a tap focuses the button, so `:focus-within` was true afterwards and silently held the sheet open — the second tap removed `is-open` and nothing moved at all. The open state keys off `:has(:focus-visible)` now, and the script off `btn.matches(':focus-visible')`, which is the distinction the platform already draws between arriving by keyboard and arriving by pointer; specificity is unchanged because `:has()` takes its most specific argument. Second, a tap fires `mouseover`/`mouseenter` immediately before `click` on real touch hardware, and `mouseenter` was calling `markOpen()` — so the very tap that closes the flyer reset the 250ms dwell clock to zero a moment before the click handler read it, meaning a tap could never pop, only soft-close. `markOpen()` now runs from `mouseenter` only where hovering is what opens it; on touch the click handler marks it. Verified against real trusted clicks in `dev/mobile-preview.html`: fifteen tap-open/tap-close pairs, every one popping with all 26 droplets.
- A close only counts as a pop if the sheet was open for 250ms first. A brush past on the way to something else shouldn't fire the whole performance. Under that dwell the flyer takes `.is-soft-close` and runs the unroll backwards over .25s instead — the rectangle returning to a burst, the artwork fading, the label coming back — with no droplets and the button back at once rather than after the pop's wait. The clock starts when it began opening, that being the span actually spent on it. This is also why the hint's delayed return moved onto `.is-popping` instead of living in the closed state: a quiet close needs its caption back with the button, not half a second later.
- That soft close is the one place a class carries closing timings, and it is safe in the direction that matters: the resting state remains the instant vanish, so a class that fails to land before the style flush merely makes the close abrupt. The reverse arrangement — gentle by default, instant on the pop class — is what used to leave the sheet shrinking in view behind the droplets.
- The flyer is inert while the pop plays. Every open-state selector is gated on `:not(.is-popping)`, so for that half second hover, focus and tap all do nothing: the button is not on screen yet, and sweeping back over the space it is about to occupy used to spring the whole sheet open out of nothing. A tap in that window is ignored rather than queued, so nothing expands a beat later in response to a click on empty space, and the moment the class lifts the flyer opens on its own if the pointer is over it. `popShut()` also refuses to run while a pop is already going — waving the cursor across the gap previously replayed the droplets over a sheet that had already left, and that replay branch existed for a case that can no longer happen.
- The button now waits .6s after the pop before reforming, instead of .14s, and assembles itself rather than appearing: a dot fades in over 120ms where the button will be, then the spikes grow out of it into the burst over 180ms — the opening morph's own trick, run from a point rather than a band and in a fifth of the time. The dot is derived, not drawn: every point of the rolled shape is pulled back onto a small ellipse about its centre along its own ray, the same projection that flattens the spikes onto the rectangle when it opens, which is why they appear to shoot out of the dot instead of the shape unfolding. So there is no third shape to keep in step — it falls out of the first two, and `dev/morph.py` is still the only thing that defines geometry. The label and the caption wait for the shape to be home at .86s; carried in on the container's fade they would sit over a full stop. The droplets are clear by ~345ms and the space then sits empty for a beat, which is what stops the whole thing reading as a blink rather than as something having popped. The hint below it waits on the same clock, or a caption is left floating under an empty space — and that in turn means the hint needs its opening transition declared separately in the open-state rules, otherwise it inherits the .5s delay and sits over the sheet for most of the unroll. The safety timeout in `popShut()` went from 900ms to 2200ms to outlast the sequence; at its old value it removed the class mid-return and cut the button off. Re-hovering during the gap cancels the wait and brings the button straight back, so the pause costs nothing in responsiveness.

### Removed
- The curled bottom edge. A 10px shaded strip sat under the sheet standing in for the paper curling where it had unwound from, which made sense while opening was a literal unroll; now that the outline morphs straight into the frame there is nothing to have unrolled from, and it read as a bar stuck to the bottom rather than as paper. Four replacements were built and compared in `dev/curl-options.html` — a properly barrel-shaded roll the sheet unwinds off, peeling corners, a hanging wave along the bottom edge, and nothing at all — and the sheet simply stopping square won. One element and its rules deleted rather than added, and it now matches the framed artwork beside it in the hero.
