# Changelog

All notable changes to the Flock Off Cobb site are recorded here. Entries are kept short;
the long-form reasoning behind older entries is in git history.

## 2026-08-31

### Added
- **"Click to open!" under the hero art, and a single nudge to go with it.** Bangers in signal red, right-aligned where the art credit used to sit, tilted the way a comic sets an aside. It reads "Tap to open!" below 860px, reusing the flyer's `.hov`/`.tap` swap.
- **The art shakes itself once, half a second after the page has settled.** A shimmy and a swell, peaking at 1.034 scale and about 6px of travel, with the hint pulsing a beat behind it so the two read as one gesture. Once per load, and skipped entirely for anyone who has already opened the reader.
  - **No rotation in it, and that is not a style choice.** The flight measures the hero and fits a stand-in onto it, and `fitPose` solves a scale and an offset — a hero caught mid-scale or mid-shift is absorbed exactly, a hero caught mid-rotation is not. Same constraint that governs the hover lift.
  - Fires on `load` + 500ms rather than on a timer from parse, so the wait is half a second of a finished page. On a slow connection a timer from parse spends it on a page that has not drawn.
  - `open()` clears the class before it measures anything, so clicking mid-nudge is clean: verified in both engines at 1.034 scale, the stand-in's first frame starts at the hero's resting `{804,161,476}` rather than at the swollen one.
  - Silent under `prefers-reduced-motion`: the class is never added at all, verified.

### Changed
- **The art credit is pencilled on the inside of the zine's cover.** Three lines where a zine's maker signs it, on the left-hand page of the spread the book opens to: the cover artist, then "Zine art and content by anonymous concerned citizens", then "Thank you".
  - Set in Caveat, the one handwriting face on the site. The zine's own hand could not do this job — VTC Letterer Pro is caps-only comic lettering, so it reads as printed *on* the page, and a signature has to read as written on it.
  - Graphite (`#6f6758` at .88), not the zine's `--z-ink`. Anything set in the ink reads as part of the printing rather than as something a person added afterwards. Each line takes a fraction of a degree of rotation and its own left edge, small enough to read as unevenness rather than as decoration.
  - **The line under the hero art is gone**, and `.art-credit` with it. The footer's legal line still names the artist, so nothing is lost for a visitor who never opens the book.
  - The art itself does not move: `{804,161,476,613}` before and after on desktop, unchanged on a phone. What moves is everything below it — the hero's text column re-centres 20px up on desktop, and on a phone, where the art sits above the text, everything under it rises by the removed line's 58px.

### Added
- **The zine is on the site, and it opens out of the hero art.** Pressing the chicken unfolds it into the fourteen-page reader; closing folds it back down onto the same picture. No page load anywhere in it.
  - Inlined into `index.html` rather than iframed or given its own page, because the flight carries clones of the real leaves so its last frame is pixel-identical to the live book, and clones do not cross documents.
  - The hero art keeps its frame, its trim, its shadow and its credit. Verified by full-page pixel diff against the committed page at 1400x950 and 390x844: the only pixels that differ anywhere on the site are the corner box, one 85x57 rectangle on desktop and 67x46 on a phone.
  - Two changes to the flight and no more: `TILT_Y` is 0 because the hero is flat and has to stay flat, and the fore-edges grow from nothing rather than starting at the full block, because a flat poster has no page edges to show.
- **The cue on the art is the comic's own corner box**, and it is the same box as the one on the cover inside — same rules, same corner, different word. "NO. 1 / Read it" on the hero, "NO. 1 / FREE" in the book, cross-fading into each other across the flight so the cue becomes the price box as the cover swings. The two opacities sum to 1.000 at every sampled frame.
- **A neutraliser above the zine's CSS**, for the leak the zine's own page never had to survive: `index.html` sets `a`, `a:hover` and `h1,h2,h3` as bare element rules and all three reach inside the reader. Headings and the resting link state go in `:where()` so they outrank a bare `h2` but lose to everything the zine writes, `.dl-title` included. The hover rule is deliberately not in `:where()` — the site's `a:hover` is (0,1,1) and would otherwise light a yellow highlight under every citation in the book.

### Changed
- **`--ink` and `--ink-soft` are `--z-ink` and `--z-ink-soft` throughout the zine's CSS.** The one real collision between the two palettes, and at `#17140f` against `#161311` it would have read as slightly wrong rather than as broken.
- **The zine's `body` rule is a `.reader,.flight` rule.** Left as a body rule it would have put a caps-only comic face and a dark board behind the whole campaign site.
- **Focus is restored when the hero is visible again, not at the end of `close()`.** The animated close hides the hero for the length of the flight, and `focus()` on a `visibility:hidden` element does nothing at all, silently. It now runs from the morph's `onDone`. Since neither engine focuses a button when it is clicked on macOS, `lastFocus` is almost always the body, and the new fallback to the hero button is the case that actually runs — without it a keyboard visitor was dropped at the top of an 8,800px page.
- **The page is locked before the hero is measured**, and unlocked before it is measured again on the way out. A precaution against classic scrollbars widening the viewport when the body's overflow is hidden, not a fix for anything observed: the scroll position is untouched either way, measured at 120 before, during and after in both engines.

### Fixed
- **The flying cover was 10px wider than the one it landed on.** `.hero-art .frame` gives the art its 5px trim and its hard shadow, and it is a descendant selector — the stand-in carries a clone of that image appended to `document.body`, with no `.hero-art` ancestor, so the rule never reached it. The cover flew with no trim and no shadow and both snapped on at touchdown. Because `box-sizing` is `border-box`, the missing 5px border also meant the artwork itself rendered 10px wider and shrank to fit on landing, which is what read as the jump.
  - Measured across the cover at mid-height: flying, ink ran x=40..515 with no trim; resting, x=45..515 inside a 5px trim starting at x=40, plus the 6px hard shadow out to x=521. After the fix all three states — flying, settled, and never-opened — measure x=40..521.
  - Selector is `.hero-art .frame,.flight .frame` now. `.flight` is the only other place that image is ever drawn.
- **The cover settled into place instead of arriving.** A small jump at the end of every close: the stand-in landed correctly and then the hero art moved. `:focus-visible` was in the hover-lift rule, `close()` hands focus back to the button at the instant the stand-in lands, and programmatic focus matches `:focus-visible` — so the art lifted 2px over .18s immediately after touchdown. The `is-settling` guard only covered `:hover` and never saw it.
  - Measured: the stand-in's last frame at x=803.99 against the hero's 804, and the hero then found at 802.12 with `translate(-1.88px,-1.88px)`, mid-lift. After the fix the landing gap is 0.01–0.06px across all three close routes (Close, Escape, pressing a cover) in both engines, with no transform on the hero.
  - The lift is hover-only now. `:focus-visible` keeps the 4px yellow outline, which is what it is for and which moves nothing.
- **Half the corner-box cross-fade never ran.** The pair's start values were written while their stylesheet transition was live — `ghost.style.transition='none'` is set on the ghost and transition does not inherit — so the price box, sitting at its default opacity of 1, was given a start value of 0, began a 780ms fade down, and was merely reversed by the rAF asking for 1 again. It never left 1 and the cue dissolved into nothing. The cue looked correct throughout because its start value is the same 1 it already had.

## 2026-08-30

### Removed
- **The caption under the closed issue.** "Public Safety or Mass Surveillance? · Open the issue", set in letterspaced mono under the cover, was the only thing on the stage that looked like a website rather than a comic sitting on a table. The stage is just the book now.
  - The label moved onto the button as an `aria-label` rather than being deleted. Without it the button's accessible name falls back to the cover art's `alt`, which describes a chicken and never mentions that pressing it opens anything.
  - What is genuinely lost is a text cue for touch, where there is no cursor and no hover lift. The judgement is that a large comic book centred on an empty stage does not need to be captioned to look pressable, but if it turns out to, the cue should be quieter than the line that was there.

### Fixed
- **The centre seam no longer snaps dark while the book opens and shuts.** A near-black band appeared down the gutter the moment the cover swung over, held for the rest of the motion, and vanished at the swap to the real book — and ran in reverse on the way out.
  - **It was a drop shadow, not the gutter shading.** A leaf carries the shadow it casts, offset right and down. In the live book that is harmless: the right-hand page is painted over it, so nearly all of it is hidden and the gutter is just the two soft `::before` gradients meeting. In the stand-in it is not — the page riding on the back of the cover sits inside `.flight-hinge` at `z-index:5`, above the right half, so its shadow landed across the seam and painted over the paper.
  - Measured down the centre column, worst point: **49 during the morph against 173 settled**. After the fix it holds 170-175 for the whole motion in both directions, which is the settled value.
  - The shadow is restated per side rather than switched off. `box-shadow:none` would take the 3px ink trim with it and the stand-in would lose its outline for the length of the morph. The cover in front of it already carries the hero's own shadow, which is the one that belongs to a board being turned.
  - Found by bisection rather than reading: hiding each part of the stand-in in turn made the seam *darker*, not lighter, which ruled out everything visible and pointed at something painting from above. Two earlier guesses — a sub-pixel gap between the halves, and the cover's own ink border — were both tested and both wrong; widening the overlap to 8px moved the number by 7.
- **The barcode was hard to hit, and missing it shut the book.** Drawn, it is 35x44 on a phone — under the 44px a thumb needs in both directions. That was survivable while a near-miss did nothing; once the back cover started closing the reader on a press, a near-miss closed the reader instead of opening the link, which reads as the barcode being broken.
  - The anchor now grows an invisible margin: a `::after` at `inset:-11px`, taking the press target to 52x62 while the block stays exactly where it was drawn against the artwork. A pseudo-element because a press landing on it still reports the `<a>` as the event target, which is what both the cover-close bail-out and the library's own `checkTarget` need to see. In px, not `cqw` — a thumb is not proportional to the page.
  - **The code itself was checked and is correct.** Its 37x37 modules were parsed back out of the SVG and compared against a freshly encoded QR at version 5, EC-M: an exact match at mask 3, so it encodes the intended archive.org URL and nothing has rotted.
  - Verified: a press 8px outside the drawn block opens the link with the reader intact, and a press elsewhere on the back cover still closes to the stage.

### Added
- **Pressing a cover shuts the book.** Three faces do it: the outside front, the inside front, and the outside back. A press returns to the closed issue on the stage with the closing morph playing; the pages between the covers still turn.
  - **The inside front cover is the case that prompted it.** A first pass only caught page 0, so pressing the blank left page of the opening spread just turned back to page 0 — leaving a closed book sitting in the middle of the reader rather than back on the stage, a state nothing else in the interface produces.
  - **The inside BACK cover is deliberately not in the set**, though it is a cover face and the symmetry is tempting. It is the right-hand page of the last spread, so pressing it is how you turn to the back cover — and while it closed the book instead, the back cover could not be reached by pressing at all. From the back, the book shuts on the back cover itself or on Close. A second pass; the symmetric version shipped for one revision and made a page unreachable.
  - The press has to be taken off the library before it sees it. StPageFlip binds `mousedown` and `touchstart` on the book and turns the page on release, so without stopping the press at capture on `document`, ahead of anything bound lower down, a cover would turn and close at once.
  - That costs the finger-following peel on these three pages, so a drag is answered directly instead: right turns back, left turns on, which is what dragging a cover does. Under 24px it is a tap, and a tap shuts the book. Every other page is untouched.
  - Anything you can actually press keeps its press — the barcode on the back cover — via a `closest('a,button')` bail-out. Presses are also ignored while a morph is running, so a double-click on the hero cannot open and immediately shut it.
  - Verified in WebKit on a phone: the back cover is reachable by pressing the page before it, all three faces close to the stage with the morph playing rather than jumping, the barcode still opens its link without turning, the sheet downloads still work, and an inner page still turns on tap.
- **The barcode says what it is**, as a note pencilled in under it. "further reading" in the zine's own hand-lettering, at low opacity in the paper's cream, centred beneath the block on the dark cover.
  - Quieter than the "you are here" arrow on page 14 on purpose. That one is a red pen interrupting the page; this only has to name what the code is, so it is a note on a dark cover rather than a correction to it.

The second put it beside the block with a small drawn arrow curving onto the code; the arrow was more voice than the line needed.

  - Decorative and `aria-hidden`: the anchor's own label already opens with "Further reading", so a screen reader would otherwise hear it twice. `pointer-events:none`, so it cannot swallow a press meant for the code — whose target is deliberately larger than it looks.
  - Verified at 1180px and on a phone: the note stays inside the leaf, never overlaps the block, and the code still opens its link with the reader intact.

Same origin, so it is a local round trip; if it fails the panel simply carries no size rather than blocking on it. The line reserves its height either way, or the buttons jump down when the number lands.

### Fixed
- **Tapping a link inside the book also turned the page.** Both the new download buttons and, it turns out, the barcode on the back cover: the link fired *and* the page flipped under your thumb.
  - StPageFlip's `checkTarget()` is `!["a","button"].includes(t.tagName.toLowerCase())` against the event's **own** target. It does not walk up the tree. The barcode is an `<a>` wrapping an `<img>` and the buttons are `<a>`s wrapping `<b>`/`<i>`, so the target reported IMG or B, the anchor was never consulted, and the turn went ahead. `clickEventForward` was doing nothing for either.
  - Fixed by making the children transparent to hit-testing, which puts the anchor back in the target slot. The barcode has had this since it was added and nobody had tapped it on a touch screen.
  - Verified on a phone in WebKit: the sheet downloads and the book stays on page 14; the barcode opens its link and the book stays on the back cover.
- **The cover no longer shows through itself as it opens.** For about 300ms of every opening the chicken went on painting after the board had passed vertical, so you saw it mirrored across the open cover. Present since the morph was written; invisible in Chrome, plain in Safari and on any iPhone, and only spotted once the morph started running on phones.
  - **`backface-visibility` does not work on this element in WebKit**, and not in the way "doesn't work" usually means. Measured across seven front/back transform pairings: with no transform on the front face it paints at *every* angle, and with any transform on it — `rotateY(0deg)`, `translateZ`, both together, either sign — it paints at *no* angle. Always on or always off, never actually culled. Chrome culls it correctly either way, which is exactly why it shipped.
  - Each CSS-only fix traded one failure for the other: adding a transform to the front stopped the show-through and left a blank cream cover for the first frames instead. Separating the faces in Z did not help, because the front was not being z-ordered behind anything — it was being culled outright.
  - **So the swap is driven from the animation instead.** A rAF loop reads the leaf's computed matrix each frame; for a `rotateY` the first matrix entry is `cos(angle)`, so its sign is the whole test, and a class toggles `visibility` on the two faces. It costs one loop for the length of the morph, is identical in both engines, and cannot disagree with what is on screen because it is derived from it. It also survives an interrupted or reversed transition, which a time-based swap would not — the zine's easing does not reach 90 degrees at the halfway point in time.
  - `backface-visibility` stays on the faces as belt-and-braces for engines that honour it. The loop is cancelled in both `finish()` and `cancel()`, so an interrupted morph does not leave it running.
  - Verified in WebKit at 390x844 and 1100x800 and in Chrome: cover art present on the right early in the swing, zero colour bleed on the left late in it, and the face swap tracking the cosine exactly (front visible while cos > 0, back once cos < 0). Closing from the back cover and from a mid-book spread both filmed frame by frame with no wrong-face flash and no errors.

### Changed
- **The book is a two-page spread at every width, phones included.** `usePortrait` was `true`, which handed narrow screens to the library's portrait mode: one page at a time, no facing page. It is off now.
  - **This deletes the mobile work rather than doing it.** Portrait had no facing page, and so no gutter shading, no fore-edges, no folio sides and no opening morph — every one of those is gated off in portrait because none of them means anything without a spread. The plan had been to write portrait equivalents of all four. Turning portrait off makes those gates unreachable instead, so a phone gets the same code path a desktop does, and 18 of the 20 leaves carry `data-side` on a phone exactly as they do on a laptop.
  - **`minWidth` had to come down from 280 to 140.** At 280 the library refuses to draw a spread narrower than 560px, so on a 390px screen it drew one 560 wide and the right-hand page simply fell off the edge — no scrollbar, no warning, just gone. 140 is a page on a 320px screen, the narrowest still worth supporting.
  - **The narrow-screen `.bookbox` cap is deleted, not adjusted.** It halved the height budget on the theory that narrow meant one page. The rule in section 3 already converts available height into a spread width and fits both axes at any size.
  - **The side padding is now the fore-edge width, not a round number.** The page-stack blocks sit at `left:100%` and `right:100%`, outside the book box, because that is where the paper they stand for is. At 8px the right-hand one ran off the screen and was clipped to a sliver. It is `calc(var(--thick) + 3px)` so it cannot drift from the thing it is making room for.
  - **What this costs, plainly: body copy is about half the size it was.** Two pages in the width that used to hold one. Roughly 6px on a 390px screen against 12px before, and about 4.6px on a 320px screen. The zine is a comic and reads as an object, the spread is the point, and the page zooms with a pinch — the viewport meta deliberately does not lock scaling. But if it turns out to read badly on a real phone, the honest fix is going back to one page at a time, not shaving the type scale.
  - Verified in real WebKit via Playwright at 390x844, 844x390, 320x568, 768x1024 and 1280x800: the book fits its viewport at all five with no horizontal scroll, and the landscape phone is comfortably the best of them. Opening morph, tap-to-turn, arrow taps, End, closing morph and reopen all exercised on a touch screen with no console or page errors, no orphaned stand-ins, and the fore-edges tracking 1/12 → 2/11 → 3/10 → 13/0 across the book.
  - **Two harness bugs nearly passed as product results, both worth knowing.** Playwright's `isMobile: true` makes WebKit derive its layout viewport from the meta tag rather than the requested size, so four different screens all reported 390x844 and looked like four passes; `isMobile: false` with an explicit viewport is the honest measurement for a page that declares `width=device-width`. And zsh does not word-split unquoted parameters, so a `set -- $dims` loop passed every case the same default size. Separately, `.book > .leaf` matches nothing — the library wraps the leaves in `.stf__wrapper` — which made a working `data-side` look broken.

  - Measured on the artwork itself rather than its frame: within 1.3px opening and 1.5px closing, against roughly 32px before.
- **The stand-in is fitted to the hero's measured rectangle, not to a calculated one.** The last visible jump was a straightforward geometry mismatch: the stand-in's cover started ~12px wider than the hero's and about 2px off, so the first and last frames of the motion did not sit on what they replaced.
  - Cause: the two use **different perspective projections**. The hero gets its from the `perspective` *property* on `.issue`, which projects from the centre of the cover. The stand-in gets its from the `perspective()` *transform function*, which projects from its own transform-origin — and applies it to an element two pages wide before scaling it down. Same 1400px, different maths, and no amount of matching the numbers makes them agree.
  - Three further sources of mismatch, all found by differencing the stand-in against the hero with `mix-blend-mode` rather than by reasoning about it:
    - **The cover is laid out at the hero's pixel size and scaled up to fill the face**, rather than laid out at page size. Not everything on a cover scales with its container: the corner box's padding is in `em` of the inherited 17px root size, its border is 2.5px, the trim is 3px. Laid out at page size and scaled down by the pose, those came out 13–21% small — the corner box 3px narrow and 6px short — while the `cqw` type beside them was exact.
    - **The perspective distance scales with the element's width.** Magnification depends on `z/P`, and `z` grows with width, so the same 1400px on a 415px-wide face falls off faster than on the hero's 360px one. Fitting the outer box then aligned the far edge and left the near edge short — the fold down the left of the cover was 3.3px shy. `perspFor()` keeps both depth ratios identical.
    - **`rotateX(3deg)` is gone.** All but invisible, and it cost 1–3px down the whole cover for the same reason, vertically.
  - Rather than reconcile the algebra, `fitPose()` measures it. Pose the stand-in, measure the box that has to land on the hero, solve the leftover as a plain screen-space scale and offset, in two passes because the offset depends on the scale. Transitions are forced off for the duration and the start pose is flushed before they are restored, so none of the measuring is ever seen.
  - Every pose is built by `poseStr()` from the same list of functions — with two leading identity slots reserved for the correction — so any two poses interpolate component by component instead of going through matrix decomposition.
  - The target is the cover's *rendered* rect, `.issue-front`, not the untransformed `.issue` box the tilt is applied to. Those are different rectangles and using the wrong one is what the analytic pose was doing.
  - Also matched the shadows: the stand-in's cover had a single 22px box-shadow against the hero's 16px drop-shadow plus a tight contact one, so the shadow changed shape at the join.
  - Measured after: opening starts within `0.6px` horizontally and under `0.3px` on every other axis; closing lands within `0.1px`. Was ~12px.
- **The page block exists everywhere now, and thins as you read.** It used to be drawn only on the closed cover, so the opening had to fade it out and the closing had to fade it back in — the paper popped out of existence on open and into existence on close, which was the last visible jump at both joins. One `.pagestack` recipe is now shared by the closed issue on the stage, both outer edges of the open book, and the stand-in that morphs between them.
  - An open book has two blocks: what you have read on the left, what is left on the right. Their widths are `--thick × (i / last)` and `--thick × (1 − i / last)`, so every turn moves one leaf of the eighteen across and the unread edge visibly thins. They ease over the same 800ms a page turn takes.
  - **The trim is inset box-shadows, not a border, and that is what makes the ends meet.** With `* { box-sizing:border-box }` a 3px border never lets the element get thinner than 3px, so a block with nothing left to show still painted a sliver: the closed issue grew a left-hand fore-edge it should not have and the last page a right-hand one, and the morph still popped by 3px at each end. An inset shadow on a zero-width box paints nothing.
  - 2px of trim rather than the cover's 3px. The block runs from 13px down to nothing, and at 4 or 5px a 3px trim leaves so little paper that the fore-edge reads as a drawn line instead of a stack of sheets.
  - The blocks are children of `.book`, not of the wrapper, so hiding the book for a morph hides them with it. StPageFlip only manages its own `.stf__wrapper`, so appending after `loadFromHTML` is safe.
  - The stand-in's blocks are siblings of the hinge rather than children of it: opening a cover does not move the paper it was wrapped around, so they hold still while the cover turns off them. The cover face therefore clones `.issue-front` alone rather than the whole `.issue-inner`.
  - `--thick` moved to `:root`, since three separate things now measure from it.
  - Measured at the joins: the hero's block is 13px and the stand-in's first frame is `0 | 13`; the book sat at `5 | 8` before a close and the stand-in's first closing frame is `5 | 8`. Exact on both sides.
- **Closing is the same motion played backwards**, and it works from any spread rather than only from the title page.
  - The swinging leaf always carries the **cover on its front and the left-hand page on its back**. Opening turns it `0 → -180deg`, so the cover leaves the right half and the blank verso arrives on the left. Closing turns it `-180 → 0`, so whatever verso you were reading rides round on the back of the cover and the cover arrives face-up on the right. That single arrangement is what lets the book shut from page 11 as readily as from page 1 — the back face just carries a clone of whichever verso is on screen.
  - `morphOpen` became `runMorph`, which takes every animated value as an argument. The leaf rotation, the gutter opacity and the page-stack opacity moved out of `.is-landing` rules and onto inline styles, because one element now runs both ways. The pose maths is shared: `heroPose()` maps the stand-in's right half onto the closed issue, and opening unwinds from it while closing winds back to it.
  - The page stack fades out over the first 45% on the way open, and back in over the *last* 45% on the way shut, so it does not reappear until the cover is nearly down on it.
  - **Closing mid-open is deliberately not animated.** The stand-in would have to start from the fully open spread, which means snapping open before it could shut. That reads worse than no animation.
  - **It closes from a lone cover too**, which it did not at first: the guard demanded two visible leaves, so reaching the back cover and closing dropped to a plain fade with no animation at all. The two lone states turn out to be exact mirrors — the back cover stands alone on the LEFT half, the front cover alone on the RIGHT — and the same construction covers both without any new geometry.
    - From the **back cover**: it is the left-hand page, so it rides round on the back of the cover exactly as a verso does, and the front cover lands on the right. No facing page, because that half genuinely is table. The fore-edge sweeps left to right under it, which is what the paper does when a book is shut.
    - From the **front cover**: it is already lying where the closed book will be, so nothing turns — the leaf stays at 0° and only the pose travels down to the stage. There is no back face at all; it never turns far enough to show one.
    - `runMorph` takes a null `recto` and a null `back` for these.
  - **The two fore-edges do not live in the same place, because they do not behave the same way.** Both were siblings of the hinge at first, which left strips of paper hanging in empty space during a close from the back cover — visible, and the thing that made the motion look wrong.
    - The **right-hand** one is the unread block. The cover swings off it and it holds still, so it stays a sibling of the hinge.
    - The **left-hand** one is the block already read, and shutting a book lifts that block and carries it over with the cover. It now lives inside the leaf's *back face* and rotates with it, going edge-on at 90° and vanishing with the rest of that face. As a sibling it stayed behind, floating off the left of the stand-in and shrinking there in full view.
    - With no facing page (`is-lone` — either cover) the right half is bare table until the cover lands on it, so the right-hand edge waits and arrives *with* the cover instead of growing into empty space ahead of it. With a facing page there is a recto sitting there throughout, the edge has something to belong to, and it simply moves.
    - Sampled across a slowed close from the back cover: the left edge travels x 55 → 226 → 521 → 690 and collapses to 0.8px exactly as its face goes edge-on; the right edge holds at 0 until 67° and then grows in to 12.2px as the cover comes down.
  - Verified over three clean open/close cycles and from spreads deep in the book: both directions animate, the hero and the book are restored, the reader ends hidden, no stand-ins are left behind, no console errors.
